## Description (≤ 4000 chars)
TuckBar tucks the macOS menu bar out of the way on the displays you choose, and brings it back the instant you reach for it.

The menu bar is bright, static, and always on. On an OLED that's burn-in fuel; on any screen it's clutter you don't always want. macOS can auto-hide the menu bar, but only for every display at once. TuckBar makes it per-display: tag the monitor you want kept clean, say your OLED, and TuckBar tucks its menu bar while every other monitor keeps its own.

And it does it seamlessly. Instead of a black band, TuckBar fills the strip with that display's actual wallpaper, captured live, so the bar simply appears to vanish. The wallpaper runs clean to the top edge. Move your pointer up there and the real menu bar slides back; move away and it tucks again. Prefer maximum protection? Switch the cover to solid black (pixels off), or use the slider to just dim the bar.

Need the bar for a while, like a screen recording or a finicky app? Untuck it for 5 to 60 minutes and it re-tucks itself. Optionally hide the Dock too while you're at it. TuckBar tucks automatically when a tagged display is connected and restores when it's unplugged, lives quietly in the menu bar, and puts everything back when you quit.

Clean screen. Happier OLED. The menu bar, only when you want it.

Requires macOS 14 or later.

## What's new in 0.2

- fixed on macOS 27: the wallpaper cover could show solid black. it now matches your desktop again, including the soft gradient macos draws at the top.
- the bar fades back the moment your pointer reaches the top edge, and tucks away softly when you leave.
- new "use the full screen" option lets windows grow into the menu-bar space (macos hides the bar on every display to allow it).
- steps aside properly under full-screen apps, and leaves the notch alone.

## What's new in v0.1
First public release. Per-display menu-bar tucking with a seamless wallpaper cover (or solid black / dim), hover-to-reveal, Untuck timer, optional Dock hiding, and tuck-on-connect.
